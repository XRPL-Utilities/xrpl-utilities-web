Understanding XRP ETFs

What an XRP ETF is

A spot XRP ETF keeps XRP with a custodian and sells shares in it on a stock exchange. A share is a claim on part of that XRP, with no wallet or key to manage. The XRP is held for all the fund's investors together.

Who issues them

In the US: Bitwise, Canary Capital, Franklin Templeton, Grayscale, 21Shares and REX-Osprey, plus basket funds from Bitwise, Grayscale, Franklin Templeton, Hashdex and 21Shares. In Europe: 21Shares, WisdomTree, Bitwise Europe, CoinShares, Virtune and Valour. In Canada: 3iQ, Purpose and Evolve. In Brazil: Hashdex.

Why correlation, not causation

Custodians hold XRP for many clients at once. When fund assets and exchange flow move the same way on the same day, that is worth noticing, but it does not show the fund caused the flow. We measure both and leave the reading to you.

Methodology

Two independent measurements, one date axis

Off-chain AUM

Fund assets are recorded once a day as each issuer publishes them. Basket funds count only their XRP share (XRP weight × fund assets).

On-chain flow

Net XRP moving out of or into known exchange wallets each UTC day, from XR-Pulse. Positive means XRP left exchanges; negative means it arrived.

Correlation, never causation

We do not tie on-chain wallets to individual funds. The rolling 7-day Pearson coefficient says whether the two series moved together, nothing more.

AUM is issuer-reported

We use each issuer's published figure as it stands. Issuers can revise or delay it.

Launch-event window analysis

For each fund, large XRP transfers in the week before launch are compared with the week from launch day. Launches older than our transfer history show no data rather than zero.
